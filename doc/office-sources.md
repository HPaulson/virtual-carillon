# Canonical-hour source audit

Canonical-hour metadata is an evidence-backed liturgical claim, not a preferred playback time. A hymn receives an `offices`/`canonicalHours` tag only when an Ordo, breviary, or an actual Liturgy-of-the-Hours resource explicitly places the hymn or antiphon in that hour. A devotional association, a tune title, or the time of day suggested by a hymn is not sufficient. The [USCCB overview of the Liturgy of the Hours](https://www.usccb.org/prayer-and-worship/liturgy-of-the-hours) is the project’s reference for the current Roman-hour terminology; the individual entries below establish the actual hymn placement.

This is an audit of the complete built-in inventory, including simple-tone variants. “Current LOTH” means a current Universalis Liturgy-of-the-Hours page; “edition-specific Office” means an identified Roman, Dominican, or monastic breviary/Office source. The latter is retained only when the source actually displays the hymn in the named hour, and its narrower scope is recorded rather than silently generalized. `Matins` is the project’s legacy label for the older Office of Readings/Matins slot; it is not a claim that the postconciliar Roman LOTH universally uses that title.

The sources below are the evidence for every currently tagged built-in hymn. The assignments are intentionally rite- and edition-specific where the source is specific; they should not be generalized to every local breviary.

The Marian antiphons are tagged `Compline` because the data model needs to represent their traditional placement after Night Prayer. They must not be described as the ordinary Compline hymn. The source review should be repeated whenever a new edition, national calendar, or rite is added.
